## SDR Persona and CRM Data Model

* Siddhant walked through a full SDR daily workflow:
  * Lead prioritization: prior contact count, company status, industry, revenue, location
  * Contact outreach prep: role, department, business email, timezone
  * Pre-call research: marketing page engagement, prior purchase history, previous call transcripts
  * Product knowledge: benefits, limitations, common customer questions
* Key distinction clarified: contacts vs. leads
  * A single company (lead) can have multiple contacts
  * Only the decision-maker with buying authority gets moved into the deals pipeline
* HubSpot demo walked through four core objects:
  * Contact, Company, Deal, Product
  * Deal fields explored: name, stage, amount, close date, owner, priority, licenses, go-live date
* SKU concept explained:
  * Unique alphanumeric identifier for the most granular version of a product
  * Example: iPhone 18 Pro Max, Burgundy, 256 GB = one SKU
  * Changing any attribute (color, storage) creates a new SKU
  * Used for inventory tracking and pricing strategy

## CRM Architecture and Build vs. Buy Framing

* CRMs are essentially fancy databases with a UI layer on top
  * Core schema: contacts, companies, deals, products
  * Frontend, backend, and everything else follows from the database design
* No existing CRM is AI-native
  * Current tools weren't designed for agents, auto-task automation, or AI-friendly data structures
  * An AI-native CRM is a real opportunity but requires a team of ~200 engineers at full depth
* Goal for this project: build a working prototype to understand effort, not to compete with Salesforce
  * Primary intent: touch-and-feel the build, then make a build vs. buy case
  * Simple edge cases (e.g., a contact moving from one company to another) reveal real complexity fast
* CRM anatomy stays largely the same across sales vs. marketing use cases; features differ, not structure
  * B2B context: sales team is almost always the primary CRM user given large deal sizes
